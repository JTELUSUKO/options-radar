How the paper account works

Entry / Now = what you paid (debit) or received (credit) for the whole trade, and what it's worth now. For credit trades it's the credit you collected and what it would cost to buy back now.
Cash goes down when you pay for a trade and up when you collect a credit. For credit trades the max loss is set aside, so Available = cash minus that collateral. A trade is refused if its max loss is more than what's available.
Exit rules (from each idea's trade plan, used when auto-close is on): credit spreads and iron condors close at +50% of the credit or when buying back costs double the credit; buy call/put and straddles at +100% or −50%; debit spreads at +60% of max profit or −50%. Everything is closed on expiry day, and anything past expiry is settled at its value at the last stock price.
Auto-trade: after a scan, opens 1 contract of each of the top N ideas once per day, skipping tickers you already hold. Win rate = closed trades with a profit.

How the ranking works

1. Are options cheap or expensive? (55% of the score)

Volatility premium (30%) — compares the 30-day implied volatility with how much the stock has actually been moving. Options usually price in more movement than happens (the best-documented edge in options), so "expensive" favours selling premium and "cheap" favours buying.
Earnings pricing (25%) — strips normal day-to-day movement out of the straddle to get the market's expected earnings move, then compares it with the stock's last 8 earnings reactions.

2. Which way? (37%)

Trend (10%) — price vs 20/50/200-day averages and 1-month momentum.
Options pricing tilt (10%) — call-minus-put implied vol at the same strikes, and how steep put skew is. Documented in research, but about half as strong as it used to be.
News & rumor (10%) — headlines from the last 7 days, scored for tone and rumor language (takeover, "in talks", "people familiar"…). Only counts fully when unusual options activity points the same way — rumors alone pay off only about a third of the time.
Unusual activity (7%) — contracts trading far above their open interest, especially short-dated out-of-the-money calls (the classic pre-takeover footprint). Low weight because free data can't tell whether trades were buys or sells.

3. Dealer gamma (filter, not a score)

Estimates how market makers hedge (assuming they are long calls and short puts). Positive gamma: hedging dampens moves, so ranges hold, which is good for selling premium. Negative gamma: hedging amplifies moves, so selling premium scores are halved. The call wall, put wall and zero-gamma lines are shown on the chart and used to place strikes, but they are not treated as price targets — research doesn't support that.

4. Picking the trade

Direction strong + options expensive → credit spread. Direction strong + options cheap → buy a call or put. Direction strong + neutral pricing → debit spread. No direction + expensive → iron condor. No direction + cheap → straddle. Strikes use ~0.25–0.40 delta, about 30 days out (or the first expiry after earnings for earnings plays), priced at the mid. Wide bid/ask and thin open interest cut the score.
